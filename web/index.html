Holdfast

Let AI trade. Never let it take unrestricted control.

Holdfast is a non-custodial vault and trading agent. An agent key can trade one pool inside limits the owner sets on chain, and it cannot withdraw. The contract enforces those limits whatever the agent or its model is told to do.

Source codeBenchmarks and methodArchitecture

Stage: testnet. No mainnet deployment or swap has happened. The contract is not audited, the live model call is untested, no outside user has tried it, and profit is not claimed.

How it works

  1. Owner

    Deploys the vault, deposits funds and sets the limits. Only the owner can withdraw.

  2. Agent

    A model turns a plain-English strategy into a typed policy. A deterministic loop then reads the pool and decides. No model runs in the trading path.

  3. Vault

    Checks every trade on chain, separately from the agent, and refuses anything outside the limits.

  4. Exchange

    Runs permitted swaps through a router fixed at deployment. Tested on a fork of the real HSKSwap router, and on our own test market on testnet.

What the limits do

The contract enforces

  • One router and two tokens, fixed at deployment
  • A cap per trade, an hourly budget and a lifetime cap, per token
  • A minimum price on every sale
  • An agent key that expires within 30 days
  • Short trade deadlines, and no replay of a trade
  • A pause, and no withdrawal by the agent

It does not prevent

  • Bad trades inside the limits. A stolen key can sell at the minimum price until a limit stops it
  • Falling prices. There is no on-chain stop-loss; the stop-loss runs in the agent, so nothing but the owner protects a position while the agent is offline
  • Gas spent by the agent key, which is separate from the token caps

Where it could go

A proposal: nothing here is built, and there are no customers or revenue. Autonomous trading is the first use. The larger product would be the vault itself, as security infrastructure for other teams' trading agents.
